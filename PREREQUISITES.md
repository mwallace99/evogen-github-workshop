# Email to send before the workshop

Send this about a week out, and again as a reminder the day before. The whole point
is that it asks for almost nothing — everything hands-on happens in a browser, so
there is no software to install and nothing that can go wrong with someone's laptop.

Resist the temptation to add to it. Every extra prerequisite is somebody who arrives
without it.

---

**Subject:** Before Thursday's GitHub session — one thing to set up (5 minutes)

Hi all,

On Thursday I'm running a session on getting your work onto GitHub, giving it a DOI
so it can be cited, and putting a simple website in front of it.

The first two-thirds I'll demonstrate — you can just watch. For the last part you'll
build your own website, live, and take it home working. That bit happens entirely in
a web browser, so **there is nothing to install**.

**Please do this before you arrive — it takes about five minutes:**

1. Create a GitHub account at <https://github.com/signup> if you don't already have
   one. Use whatever email you like, but be aware your username becomes part of your
   website address, so `jsmith` is a better long-term choice than `bumblebeeluvr92`.
2. Verify your email address. GitHub sends a confirmation link — click it. Websites
   won't publish on unverified accounts, and this is the single most common reason
   someone gets stuck.
3. Log in on the laptop you're bringing, and make sure your browser remembers you.

**Optional, only if you'd like your site to look finished on the day:** bring a
square-ish photo of yourself and two or three sentences about your research, in a
file you can copy and paste from. You can always add these later.

**Bring:** a laptop and your charger. Any operating system is fine. If you can't
bring a laptop, come anyway and pair up with someone — watching is genuinely useful.

That's everything. No software, no command line, no accounts other than GitHub.

If step 1 or 2 gives you trouble, email me before Thursday rather than on the day and
we'll sort it in two minutes.

See you then,
Matthew

---

## Notes for you, not for the email

**Why no `git` or `gh` in the prerequisites.** Installing git, installing the GitHub
CLI, and running `gh auth login` is a twenty-minute detour for a beginner, and it
fails in a different way on every machine. The browser path reaches the same finished
website. People who want the command line will ask, and you can point them at
`COMMANDS.sh` afterwards.

**Chase non-responders.** Anyone who hasn't confirmed they have an account by the day
before will turn up without one. Account creation plus email verification eats about
eight minutes of the session, and it is always at least one person. Consider arriving
fifteen minutes early and saying so in the reminder.

**The verification step is the one that actually bites.** GitHub Pages silently
refuses to publish for unverified accounts, and the failure gives no useful message —
the site just never appears. If someone's site won't build on the day, check this
first.

**Have two or three spare GitHub accounts** you control, logged in on a spare laptop
or in private browsing windows. If someone's account is broken beyond quick repair
they can drive one of yours and still leave with a finished site to look at.
